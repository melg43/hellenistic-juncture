# Introduction: The Visual Culture of the Hellenistic World

The Hellenistic period (323–31 BCE) transformed Mediterranean urban landscapes. Terraced architecture, monumental scale, and dynamic theatricality redefined civic and sacred spaces. This essay explores how the Attalid dynasty used urban planning and sculpture at Pergamon to legitimize their royal status.

## Section 1: Topography and the Theatre Terrace

Unlike the classical Athenian Acropolis, Hellenistic Pergamon utilized a dramatic, fan-shaped terracing system. The theater built into the steep hillside served as both a functional spectacle space and a visual anchor for the city.

* **Architectural Innovation:** High verticality and sightlines.
* **Political Message:** Total control over the natural landscape.

## Conclusion

The architecture and material culture of Pergamon demonstrate that Hellenistic archaeology cannot be studied in isolation. The deliberate use of drama, scale, and interconnected trade networks allowed the Attalid kings to project power across the Mediterranean world.
